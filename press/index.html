Summary · 300 words

For a feature piece, a press release lead, or the body of a review.

Petpet is a new iPhone and iPad app that treats a pet’s life the way a journal treats a decade: one quiet page at a time, kept for the person who was there. It is not a feed, not a social network, and not a medical records app. There is no sign-up, no account to create, and no Petpet-owned backend. Everything a user writes lives in their own iCloud, encrypted at rest by Apple, and nowhere else.

The app is built around a warm, day-grouped Timeline with seven kinds of entries — note, photo, weight, vaccine, medication, mood, and walk. A Photos library groups attachments by month with quick filters. Insights chart weight over time, remind owners when vaccines come due, and track medication schedules. A Monthly Recap renders a soft 9:16 share card that summarizes the month a pet and their person just lived together. On the Home Screen, a widget and a Live Activity turn the phone into a quiet companion rather than a demand for attention.

The flagship Pro feature is the Lifetime Book: a printable PDF yearbook rendered entirely on-device — cover, dedication, a page per entry, the walks and weight chapters, a closing colophon. It is a small inversion of the everything-is-ephemeral posture of the modern pet app category: a pet’s life, shaped to be kept.

Petpet is free for a single pet with every core journaling feature. Pro unlocks multiple pets, the Lifetime Book export, the Home Screen widget, the Live Activity for walks, an optional AI Poetize writing tool (bring-your-own OpenRouter key), and Advanced Insights. Pro is $3.99 per month with a 7-day free trial, $29.99 per year, or $79.99 once for life. An Early Supporter Lifetime at $49.99 is offered to the first wave.

Three things we promise

Private. No Petpet-owned backend. If the user turns iCloud sync on, their journal goes to their Apple ID container only. HealthKit is read-only and never leaves the device. No ads, no trackers, no social feed, no followers.

Lifetime. Built for the ten-plus years a person has with their pet, not for a seven-day streak. No push notifications badgering the user. No vanity numbers. No growth hacks.

Yours. The Lifetime Book PDF export means a user can print their pet’s book, email it to their mother, or keep it on a shelf. It is their copy, not something hosted on a server that could disappear.

Why we built it

Petpet started with a frustrating conversation. A friend who’d had his dog for ten years couldn’t tell me what she weighed at age three. Not because he didn’t care — because he loved her — but because that number was buried somewhere between a text to his vet, a photo in his camera roll, and a receipt he’d long since lost.

Ten years. Nine thousand photos. Nothing he could actually hold. So we’re building the thing he wished he had.

— Xun Wang, founder

Feature highlights

Warm daily Timeline. Seven kinds of entries (note, photo, weight, vaccine, medication, mood, walk) grouped by day. No endless scroll — only days a pet has pages for.

Photos, by month. Every photo lives inside its moment. Filter by firsts, outdoor, food, sleep, or vet visits.

Insights. Weight charts (Swift Charts), vaccine due-dates, medication streaks. No social metrics, no vanity numbers.

Monthly Recap. A soft 9:16 share card summarizing the month a pet and their person just lived together.

Lifetime Book. Export everything as a printable PDF yearbook. Cover, dedication, a page per entry, the walks and weight chapters, a closing colophon. Pages are rendered on-device via PDFKit; nothing is uploaded.

Walks together. With HealthKit permission, Petpet reads step count and walking distance and frames them as time spent beside a pet. Read-only; the data never leaves the device.

Widget & Live Activity. A quiet home-screen companion. A walk timer on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island.

AI Poetize (optional, BYOK). A writing tool that gently rewrites a short note in a lyrical voice. Users bring their own OpenRouter API key; requests go directly from the device to the model they chose. Petpet keeps no log.

Technical details

Stack. SwiftUI + Core Data + NSPersistentCloudKitContainer + CKAsset + Swift Charts + PDFKit + WidgetKit + ActivityKit.

Storage. Local Core Data store. Optional iCloud sync writes to the user’s own Apple ID container only; Petpet operates no server.

Privacy posture. Six data types declared on the App Store Privacy Nutrition Label: Purchase History, Health, Photos or Videos, Other User Content, Product Interaction (opt-in), and Crash Data (opt-in). All marked “Not Linked to You” and “Not Used to Track You.”

Analytics. TelemetryDeck + Sentry, both opt-in and OFF by default. 16 categorical events, no PII.

Deployment. iOS 17.0, iPhone + iPad.
